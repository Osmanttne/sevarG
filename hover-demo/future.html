Training Data

Our computer vision model is trained on diverse architectural accessibility features. These training images represent the three key categories our system learns to identify: sidewalks, ramps, and elevators. Each category helps the model understand different aspects of accessible building design.

Sidewalk Detection

Training images of sidewalks help our model identify pedestrian pathways, curb cuts, and ground-level accessibility features. The model learns to recognize width, surface texture, and obstacle-free paths.

Ramp Recognition

Ramp training data teaches the model to identify inclined surfaces, handrails, and proper gradients. This enables detection of wheelchair-accessible entrances and transitions between elevation levels.

Elevator Identification

Elevator training images help the model recognize vertical accessibility solutions. The system learns to identify elevator doors, control panels, and entrance configurations for multi-story accessibility.

Workflow

Vision for Automated Accessibility Analysis

We as a group developed our own unique set of training data for training image processing models to identify and classify architectural accessibility features. Our goal is to create a system that can automatically analyze images of buildings and infrastructure to assess their accessibility compliance.

Features

  • Training data Large arrays of images carrying different visual attributes of disabled accessibility freindly architecture
  • Strong Annotation Many Annotations made on training data and many objects defined to optimize the object detection process as best as possible
  • Augmentation Training data images are optimized for best algorithmic performance, this includes opperations such as image augmentations
  • Yolov8 Bounding box analysis and labeling through neural network models such as yolov8
  • Predictive Strength: Results are gathered and classified by confidence thresholds indicating if the algorithm had performed detections to a high degree of confidence

Impact and Timeline

Although we have made our training data and trained the Roboflow Yolov8 model with high levels of confidence in analysis, implementing this feature into sevarG is part of our plans for the future. This implementation will enable architects, building managers, and accessibility advocates to quickly assess structures.
